About Turbine Lab

Turbine Lab v1.1.0

Turbine Lab shows the three classic water turbines — Pelton, Francis and Kaplan — as 3D models you can turn, take apart and run. It is meant for anyone learning how hydropower works: students, teachers, or the curious.

The models

The models were built in Blender and exported to glTF; every part keeps its name, so the page can label it, light it up and move it. The Pelton scene is a high-head plant cut into a mountain; the Francis scene is a low weir with a powerhouse; the Kaplan scene is an in-stream unit moored on a river bed. The Francis and Kaplan scenes also hold a “wind twin”: the same machine scaled up and fed by air.

The physics

Pelton and Francis turbines are driven by head: power is P = ρ·g·Q·H·η. The in-stream Kaplan and the wind twins are driven by the speed of the stream: P = ½·ρ·A·v³·Cp. The Pelton bucket-speed curve comes from Euler's turbine equation; runner speeds come from the jet speed, the peripheral-speed ratio or the tip-speed ratio, and the specific speed Ns = n·√P / H^1.25 is checked against the band where each kind of runner works well. The physics is a small, tested module (src/physics.js).

The drawings

The technical drawings are rendered in Blender from the same models (tools/render_drawings.py): the machine is cut through the runner centre along three planes, the cut faces are capped and hatched, and Freestyle draws the visible edges. The page lays the views out on an A3 sheet in first-angle projection, with dimensions, a parts list and a title block, and can print or download it.

What it leaves out

Efficiency curves and selection-chart zones are typical shapes, not data for a particular machine. Pipe losses, cavitation, and the details of blade design are not modelled. The wind twins are thought experiments, not designs.
