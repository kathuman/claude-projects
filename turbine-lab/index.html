    No turbine is equally good at every flow. The curves show the typical share of the water's power each kind turns into shaft power as the flow drops below its rating. The marker is the machine you are running.

    Pelton only: how fast should the buckets move? A bucket standing still takes no work from the jet; a bucket as fast as the jet never gets hit. Euler's turbine equation puts the best in between — at half the jet speed. Move the "Bucket speed" slider and watch the marker.

    Curve shapes are typical for each kind of machine (as in the ESHA small-hydro guide), not those of a particular turbine. "Propeller" is a Kaplan runner with its blades locked.

    Engineers choose a turbine from two numbers: the head (how far the water falls) and the flow (how much of it). Click anywhere on the chart to place your site.

    Zones are approximate, like those on manufacturers' selection charts; near a border more than one kind works and the choice turns on cost. Dashed lines: electrical power at 90% efficiency.

    The wind twins are thought experiments built from the same parts, not real products: real wind turbines use long, slender open blades because air carries so little energy per cubic metre.
