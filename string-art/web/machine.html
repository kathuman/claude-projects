How the machine winds

The board lies on a turntable. Above its rim sits a thread guide, a thin tube with the thread running through it from a braked spool; the guide only moves in and out along one radius, and up and down. To wind a line to the next nail:

  1. Turn. The board turns until the gap beside the next nail is under the guide. The guide waits just inside the ring, so the thread from the last nail lies across the open middle and cannot catch.
  2. Out. The guide moves outward through the gap, past the nails.
  3. Wrap. The board turns by one nail, carrying the nail past the guide, so the thread goes behind it.
  4. In. The guide comes back inside through the gap on the other side. The thread is now wrapped round the nail and pulled tight by the spool brake.

Each later wrap on the same nail is laid a little higher, so they stack neatly. The axes in the G-code: X is the board's turn in degrees, Y the guide's distance from the nail circle in mm (negative inside), Z its height above the board.
