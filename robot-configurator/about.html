What it does

You describe the robot you want: a wheeled rover, a desktop arm, or a rover carrying an arm, with its payload, speed, slope, runtime or reach, and how you like to build and shop. The configurator returns designs you can build from parts sold in Denmark, Sweden or Norway, each with a landed-cost budget, step-by-step building instructions, the wiring, and two Arduino programs that compile as delivered.

It is meant for makers, students and teachers who want a robot that works the first time, and for anyone who wants to see why one part choice works and another does not.

How it works

  • Every compatible combination of catalog parts is checked against engineering limits: motor torque on the slope, tyre grip, top speed, driver and regulator voltage and current, battery discharge, runtime, logic levels, free pins, servo holding torque at the required reach, and tipping.
  • The designs that pass are priced as landed cost for your country: each parcel's shipping, VAT, IOSS or VOEC, import duty and the carrier's clearance fee, choosing the vendor mix exactly.
  • Three proposals come first: the lowest cost, the most headroom (the largest margin on the tightest check, for at most twice the lowest cost), and the quickest delivery. A chart shows every design by cost and headroom; any point can be picked, and any part of a design swapped for another to see what it costs and which check it fails.
  • When nothing fits, it says which checks block the closest designs and how far each requirement would have to move.

Limits

  • The models are first-order engineering models with safety factors, not a simulation. Cheap cells below their label, stiff gearboxes and flexible frames all cost margin, which is what headroom is for.
  • A price is either checked on the shop's own page on the date shown, or an estimate. Either way it is not a quote: check each item on the vendor's site before ordering.
  • Arm checks are static: fast moves with a heavy payload add inertial torque.
  • Stationary arms use enclosed, CE-marked mains adapters. The configurator never asks you to wire mains voltage.

Languages

The page, the checks, the budget, the building guide and the wiring are in English, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian and Spanish. The Arduino programs and their comments stay in English, like the Arduino IDE they are read in. Part names are the ones the shops use.

Versions

  • 2.2.0 (2026-10-23): proven numbers. A validation page runs the engine live on cases with a known answer: the motor, drive, battery and arm models by hand, every tax rule, the vendor optimiser against brute force and the suggested relaxations. The self-test prints a RESULT line, and a new panel sets what you measured on your built robot beside the model, says what a difference usually means, and can send the numbers in as a GitHub issue.
  • 2.1.0 (2026-10-23): see it before you buy. A drawing of the robot from its parts: the rover from above with every board and the battery placed on the chassis plate, a second deck when they do not fit, and the power wiring along that layout; the arm from the side with its home pose and reach. A build pack puts the drawing, parts and budget, wiring and building guide on one page to print or save as PDF.
  • 2.0.0 (2026-10-23): a catalog you can check. Every price is either checked on the shop's own product page, with the link and the date, or marked as an estimate with a search link; a weekly job follows every link and lists prices older than 90 days. Shops that do not sell a part lose the offer, with the reason recorded. New: packs you assemble from a holder and protected cells, chassis kits with their motors and wheels, a 5 V buck-boost regulator, an RC-hobby shop for LiPo packs and chargers, parts sold in packs, and a choice to use checked prices only.
  • 1.1.0 (2026-10-22): a cost–headroom chart with the frontier, picks from the chart, part swaps with the check that stops each alternative, a side-by-side comparison, a most-headroom proposal that really differs from the cheapest, the blueprint look of the other projects, and five languages.
  • 1.0.0 (2026-10-22): requirements to designs for rovers, desktop arms and rovers with arms; landed-cost pricing in Denmark, Sweden and Norway; building guide, wiring and Arduino programs built in CI.
