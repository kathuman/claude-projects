How far can you trust the numbers?

Every case below runs the app's own solver (src/lbm.js) on a problem with a known answer — an exact solution, a published result, or a conservation law — and checks the answer against a stated tolerance. The results shown were recorded with node tube-flow-sim/tests/validation.test.js; press Re-run to compute them again in your browser, from the same code.

GPU solver: same answer as the CPU solver

The WebGPU solver (src/lbm-gpu.js) is a port of the same scheme. Each case runs both for 400 steps and compares every node: velocity within 5·10⁻⁴ of the speed (float32 summation order), density within 10⁻⁵, the drag on the body within 0.01%, the wall force within 0.5% (a sum of thousands of nearly cancelling terms), mass within 10⁻⁶.

Limits — what the numbers don't cover

  • Resolution. A body needs at least ~6 cells across for drag within a few percent (at 4.8 cells the Stokes drag is 12% low); the app says when a case's Reynolds number is beyond what the grid can resolve and runs the highest it can instead.
  • No turbulence model. Flows are resolved directly; wakes become under-resolved well before transition in open flow (Re ~ 10³).
  • Weak compressibility. Lattice Boltzmann is weakly compressible: the app keeps the Mach number ≤ 0.17 (errors ~ Ma² ≈ 2–3%).
  • Confinement. Every case is in a tube. Published open-flow data (Schiller–Naumann, Johnson & Patel) are for comparison: blockage raises the drag and delays wake instabilities.
  • Start-up. A run starts from the inflow profile with the body present, which gives a brief force spike and pressure waves that die out over one to two flow-throughs; the measurements average over windows after that.
