    A pendulum takes the same time for every swing, whether the swing is wide or narrow (as long as it is small), and that time depends only on its length. The clock counts swings: everything else in it only carries power to the pendulum and counts.

    T = 2π √(L / g)
    Pendulum length L

    L is measured from the suspension to the bob's centre of oscillation; g = 9.80665 m/s². The calculator does not change the model: it shows what a different pendulum would do to this clock.

    The escapement is where the spring's steady push meets the pendulum's regular swing. It does two jobs at once: it lets the train move only one step per beat, and it gives the pendulum a small push each beat to make up for friction and air.

    1. Locked. A tooth rests on the locking face of one pallet. That face is an arc centred on the anchor's pivot, so as the pendulum swings on, the tooth just slides along it and the wheel stays dead still — no recoil. That is what “deadbeat” means.
    2. Unlock and impulse. Near the middle of the swing the pallet slides clear of the tooth. The spring's force turns the wheel, and the tooth pushes across the pallet's slanted impulse face: a small push to the anchor, through the crutch, to the pendulum.
    3. Drop and lock again. The wheel has turned half a tooth when another tooth drops onto the other pallet's locking face and stops. One beat is done; the next swing repeats it on the other side.

    The pallets in this model are shaped from the motion itself: seen from the swinging anchor, the tip of the engaged tooth draws the locking arc while the wheel is locked and the impulse face while it turns, so every tooth meets the pallets exactly. The forces are not simulated: the pendulum swings the same whatever the spring's pull.

    Each mesh pairs a big wheel with a small pinion, so every arbor turns faster than the one before it, and in the opposite direction. The pendulum sets how fast the escape wheel may turn; the ratios fix every other speed.

      How to make every one of these gears →

      The mainspring is the only source of energy. It pushes on the barrel all the time; the escapement lets that push out in small steps, and almost all of it ends up as heat from friction in the pivots and teeth and as the pendulum's push against the air.
