About the app

Epicycles

Any closed line can be drawn by circles turning on circles. Epicycles turns a picture into one closed line and draws it with two chains of circles: the chain above the picture sets x, the chain to its left sets y. It starts with one circle per side, then two, then three, so you can watch the drawing take shape term by term.

What it does

  • Takes any pictureText you type, a heart, a star, a gear, your own drawing, or an uploaded picture. An uploaded picture is read in the browser tab and never leaves your device.
  • Makes one closed lineIt finds the picture's outline (for logos, text and silhouettes) or its edges (for photos, ignoring their grain), choosing which from the picture and noticing a dark background, thins them to a set number of points and joins them into a single loop. A nearest-neighbour tour is improved with 2-opt until no reversal shortens it, so the line does not cross itself needlessly. Separate shapes are joined by straight jumps.
  • Draws it with circles, one count at a timeOne full loop with 1 circle per side, then 2, 3 … 10, 12, 15, 20, up to 500, with earlier loops kept faded. Or fix the count, or click the error chart to jump to any count. Circles can be added in series order or largest first. Zoom and move around while it draws, or let the view follow the pen to watch the smallest circles up close.
  • Shows every numberThe distance from the traced line for every count, as a chart and a table; the equations with their first terms; and each circle's radius, starting angle and speed. The curve at any count can be downloaded as SVG.

How it is built

The line is resampled to 1,024 points evenly spaced along its length, and each coordinate is expanded on its own as a Fourier series, x(t) = a₀ + Σ rk cos(k t + φk). Term k is one circle: radius rk, starting angle φk, turning k times while the pen goes round once. The error is exact, by Parseval's theorem.

Taylor or Fourier? A Taylor series in time cannot close a loop, but with z = eit the same sum is Re Σ Akzk, a power series in z. Each power zk goes round k times as fast, so a term's place in the expansion sets its circle's speed.

The tests check that a circle needs exactly one circle per side, that the full series rebuilds a square, that the error never grows as circles are added and equals the measured distance, that the chain tips equal the series, that the tour visits every point once and 2-opt never lengthens it, and that a drawn ring is traced along its edges. Everything runs in your browser.
