How it works

  1. Simplify first. An uploaded picture opens in the Prepare picture window. An edge-preserving (bilateral) filter flattens texture, posterizing leaves a few tone levels, a flood fill from the border turns the background pure white, and a tone curve with a hard cutoff and a gamma above 1 makes light areas white too, so texture, background and light shading do not become scribble.
  2. One closed line. The picture is reduced to pixels on its outline (an Otsu threshold) or on its edges (Canny-style: blurred, thinned to one-pixel lines, with the threshold set well above the photo's grain), or placed as shading: points as dense as the picture is dark, by error diffusion. An uploaded picture with two clear tones is traced by its outline, any other by its edges, and a dark background is detected from the border. These are thinned on a grid and joined into one cycle: nearest neighbour first, then 2-opt, which reverses any stretch whose reversal makes the line shorter, so crossings go away. Separate shapes get joined by straight jumps, because a single line has to visit them all.
  3. Even steps. The cycle is resampled to 1,024 points evenly spaced along its length. A parameter t runs from 0 to 2π once round.
  4. One series per side. Each coordinate is expanded separately: x(t) = a₀ + Σ rk cos(k t + φk), and the same for y(t). Each term is one circle. Chained end to end above the picture, the circles' tip sets x; chained down the left, it sets y. The guide lines from the two tips meet at the pen.
  5. One, two, three … With one circle per side the pen draws an ellipse, the best single-frequency fit. Each extra circle adds detail at a higher speed. The error falls toward zero as the count rises.

Taylor or Fourier?

These turning circles are a Fourier series. A Taylor series in time, c₀ + c₁t + c₂t² + …, never comes back to where it started, so it cannot draw a closed loop. Still, the two ideas are closely related. Write z = eit, a point going round the unit circle once per cycle. Then x(t) = Re(A₀ + A₁z + A₂z² + A₃z³ + …) with Ak = rkeiφk. That is a Taylor (power) series in z. Each power zk is a point going round k times as fast, so the term's place in the expansion sets its circle's speed, and its coefficient sets the circle's size and starting angle.

Prepare the picture

Make the picture simple before it becomes a line: texture and background turn into scribble, so flatten them first. The previews update as you go.

1Simplify

An edge-preserving (bilateral) filter: flattens skin, fabric and foliage, keeps the edges between different colours.
Posterize: 4–6 levels turn soft shading into a few clear areas.

2Background

Colours connected to the picture's border, and this close to it, become pure white. Raise it for a busy or shaded background; lower it if the subject is eaten.
Click a leftover patch of background in the Prepared picture to remove it too.

3Tone curve

Anything lighter than this becomes pure white and gets no points, instead of a sparse sprinkle.
Above 1, light tones get lighter while dark ones stay dark, so the line gathers where the picture is darkest.
