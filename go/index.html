How to play

Players alternate placing a stone of their colour on a line intersection; Black first. A stone or solidly-connected group is captured and removed the moment it has no liberties — empty points directly adjacent to it. You may not play a move that leaves your own stone with no liberties (suicide) unless it captures. The ko rule forbids a move that would recreate the board position from just before your opponent's last move, so a single stone cannot be recaptured immediately.

Either player may pass instead of playing. Two passes in a row end the game and open the scoring phase: click any group you both agree is dead to remove it, then press Finish. Score is by area — your stones on the board plus the empty points your stones alone surround — and White adds the komi. Highest total wins.

Choose 9×9 for a quick game, 13×13 for a medium one, or the full 19×19. The computer opponent plays a fast heuristic — solid for a casual game, not a match for a strong player. Wood and coordinate settings are cosmetic and remembered between visits.

How the code is built

The whole game is this one HTML file — no build step, no libraries. The JavaScript is a single IIFE split into labelled sections. Three layers that never reach across each other:

The rules (BOARD LOGIC) — pure functions over a flat Int8Array of 0 / 1 / -1. groupAt flood-fills a connected group and its liberties; tryMove returns the resulting board and captured stones, or null if the move is illegal (occupied, suicide, or ko). Ko is enforced by comparing against a hash of the previous position.

The brain (AI) — also pure. scoreMove rates a candidate by stones captured, liberties gained, self-atari avoided, and a distance-to-stones influence term; chooseMove picks among the best with a little noise, and strength just widens or narrows that window. It passes only once the opponent has passed and it sees no move that helps.

The view (RENDER) — rebuilds one inline SVG from the board each frame: grid lines, star points, stones with a soft shadow, a ring on the last move, and, in the scoring phase, territory dots and dead-stone marks. Undo is whole-board snapshots pushed to a stack, not reversed moves.
